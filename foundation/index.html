SCIENTIFIC & STEM FOUNDATION

Why the engineering profile did not start with software.

Biotechnology, analytical chemistry, laboratory work, STEM mentoring and engineering education formed the domain knowledge, method discipline and technical communication skills that later converged in scientific instrumentation and software.

01 / SCIENCE

Biotechnology & analytical chemistry

Formal scientific training established the measurement vocabulary behind the current engineering work: analytical methods, laboratory discipline, sample preparation, signal interpretation and the distinction between a numerical result and defensible evidence.

02 / LAB

Analytical laboratory practice

Hands-on laboratory work added the operator perspective: procedures must survive real samples, instrument setup, timing, calibration, repeated measurements and reporting constraints.

03 / STEM

WorldSkills, competitions & mentoring

WorldSkills / professional-skills practice, National Technology Olympiad participation and STEM mentoring developed a repeatable way to decompose complex systems into teachable, observable and assessable actions.

04 / PROTOTYPING

CAD, 3D & physical prototyping

CAD and 3D modelling connected abstract design with manufacturable geometry, tolerances, assembly and the practical consequences of physical interfaces — a useful bridge into instrumentation work.

05 / COMMUNICATION

Technical communication & method design

Teaching, documentation and conference work trained the same core skill used in systems engineering: turn tacit expert knowledge into explicit structures, instructions, diagrams, acceptance criteria and reusable artifacts.

06 / AI

AI / LLM in engineering education

Work with LLM-assisted educational and engineering workflows evolved into a stricter principle: agents are useful when specifications, state, permissions and evidence are machine-readable and human-controlled.
